The Mission

Cloudseeker rides a shared launch, performs a Venus transfer, and deploys an acid-resistant balloon near 55 km. We measure aerosols, gases (incl. ammonia/phosphine lines), winds, and validate materials for long-duration aerostats and future AI-assisted habitats.

  • Acid-resistant aerostat (fluoropolymer film) at 0.5–1.0 bar, ~0–30 °C.
  • Sensor suite: 4K imaging, mini-MS, chem sensors, meteo.
  • 3U CubeSat relay for high-reliability comms.
  • AI-optimized ops for navigation and anomaly response.

Astrophysics & Trajectory Engineering Deep-Dive

We target a Hohmann-like Earth→Venus transfer with a nominal ~146-day time of flight. From ~200 km LEO, the trans-Venus injection is ~3.5 km/s. We’ll buy the high-energy drop-off via rideshare/kick stage so Cloudseeker carries only ~150–200 m/s for course correction and contingency.

  • Earth v∞ ~2.5 km/s; Venus v∞ ~2.7 km/s.
  • Atmospheric entry ~10.7 km/s; guided aeroshell → balloon deployment near 55 km.
  • Dry mass target 10–12 kg (probe + 3U relay). Keep it light, keep it affordable.

Mass & Propellant Strategy

With purchased injection, course-correction Δv of 150–200 m/s needs roughly ~0.8–1.1 kg of green monoprop (Isp ~235 s) at these masses. Self-injection from LEO (~3.5 km/s) would balloon propellant (25–50 kg chemical) or push us to electric propulsion with more power/ops complexity. For a <$500k crowd mission, buying injection is the deterministic path.

Float Regime & Lifetime

  • Altitude 50–55 km, 0.5–1.0 bar, 0–30 °C.
  • Constraints: sulfuric acid corrosion, power storage, link geometry.
  • Ops goal: 30–90 days depending on materials and power budget.

Why Venus vs Mars

Venus Edge

  • Gravity: 0.903g—near-Earth; better for long-term health.
  • Atmosphere: Dense, resources; 50–55 km is Earth-like pressure/temperature.
  • Solar: Top-of-atm ~1.9× Earth (diffused in clouds but harnessable via tethers/arrays).

Known Challenges

  • Acid clouds & corrosion; choose materials/coatings wisely.
  • Radiation in clouds is non-zero; design for shielding and ops time.
  • Cloud opacity & geometry complicate comms—hence the 3U relay.

Venus Logistics & FAQ

How long does it take?

  • Hohmann-like: ~146 days (≈4.8 months) Earth→Venus.
  • Faster educational transfers: 120–180 days (higher Δv).
  • Future VenusExpress concept: 30–45 days with advanced propulsion (e.g., nuclear-electric/VA-SIMR-class), pending tech maturity.

Launch Windows

  • Windows repeat on the Earth–Venus synodic cycle (~584 days ≈ 19 months).
  • Our simulator shows phase angle and an “arrival miss” metric that drops near zero in good windows.

Comms & Light-Time

  • Earth–Venus distance varies widely; one-way light-time is typically ~2–15 minutes.
  • CubeSat relay smooths downlink and improves coverage during float ops.

Entry, Descent, Float

  • Entry speed ~10.7 km/s; aeroshell manages heating to balloon deploy at ~55 km.
  • Float regime: 0.5–1.0 bar; 0–30 °C; acid droplets present.
  • Lifetime target: 30–90 days, gated by corrosion, power and link geometry.

Materials & Power

  • Balloon: fluoropolymers & coatings for acid resistance.
  • Power: solar + storage; Venus has high top-of-atm flux; in-clouds are diffuse but workable.

Regulatory

  • Inert, sealed DNA artifacts only; no release to the environment.
  • Compliance with launch licensing and export regs; sponsor content policy enforced.
